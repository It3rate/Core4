### Axioms of Multi-Dimensional Structural Fusions

**Axiom 1: Sites and Open Potential**
A site is a referencable place capable of holding a relation. It consists of a carrier identity, a location measure, and a relation state. Initially, an isolated site holds open, uncalibrated potential (0/0) rather than an implicit zero.

**Axiom 2: Pinning**
To establish a reference, a specific site must be selected and pinned. Pinning does not invent math or create a basis; it simply privileges a stable site to act as an origin for future interactions.

**Axiom 3: Relation and Intervals**
A relation is formed by establishing an ordered coupling between a pinned 
origin site and a second site. This yields a live directed interval, 
an orientation, and a candidate denominator.

**Axiom 4: Freezing and Basis (The Unit)**
When a live relation is stabilized and committed for reuse, it is frozen. Freezing creates a basis (a unit)—a portable denominator with a fixed magnitude that persists independently of changing values.

**Axiom 5: Attachment and the Zero-Dominant**
An attachment occurs when live sites are read through a frozen basis. If the basis is too weak to interpret the relation (e.g., encountering orthogonal energy), the dominant side reads as 0. This m/0 state is not an error; it is unresolved attachment potential carrying tension that seeks structural resolution.

**Axiom 6: Structural Hardening**
Structure is not a separate layer on top of attachment. As open sites repeatedly accept data and gather tension, relations begin to prefer specific stable values. The dominant side moves away from 0, hardening into a frozen basis that can later relax back toward open potential.

**Axiom 7: Fused Structure vs. Sequential Data**
Because units possess direction, they establish opposing perspectives. Recording multiple values against a single basis produces sequential data, requiring external rules to interpret. Combining attachments with differing or opposing bases internalizes these rules into a physical, self-describing fused structure.

**Axiom 8: The Directed Segment (1D Dual-Unit)**
Fusing two opposing units—one recessive and one dominant—creates a stable dual-unit. This structure natively represents a bounded range or directed segment.

**Axiom 9: Orthogonality and 2D Fusions**
Fusing two independent, orthogonal dual-units defines higher-grade 2D structures:
*   **Area:** Fusing aligned orthogonal units generates positive or negative bounded area.
*   **Complex Rays:** Fusing opposed orthogonal units forces the axes against each other, generating complex rays that define length and angle rather than area.

**Axiom 10: Multi-Dimensional Change**
To fully describe structural change across two dimensions, a system requires four values forming two directed segments (one structural dual-unit per orthogonal axis) to capture both the start and end states of the entire dimensional plane.
